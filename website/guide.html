The guide

ChronoNote has no hidden format and no settings you're required to learn before it's useful — but a handful of typed tokens do a lot of work once you know them. This page covers all of it: the vocabulary, every shortcut, and the workflows they add up to.

The short version: notes are one plain .txt file per day. Type a token at the start of a line and it turns into a glyph on screen while the file on disk keeps the literal characters you typed — always. Nothing here is a special save format; open any note in Notepad and it reads exactly the same.

Vocabulary

Every token, and what it means

Each is at most three characters, typed at the start of a line.

Type this See this Meaning
# ☐︎ Open action. Something still to do. Closing a note dated today or earlier asks first while one of these is unresolved; a note dated in the future closes without asking.
v ☑︎ Done. Resolved.
> » Deferred. Resolved for today, but pushed forward to a later note — used when you copy/forward a task to a future day. Like done and won't-do lines it is drawn dimmed, so open work stands out.
x ☒︎ Won't do. Resolved, but distinct from done — this one just isn't happening.
- or * • Bulleted list item. Purely structural — not an action, doesn't block anything. Indent two spaces to nest.
1. 2) 1.1. 1. Numbered list item. Plain text, no glyph, and not an action. Enter continues with the next number (1. → 2., 1.1. → 1.2.), an empty item ends the list, Tab nests it by two spaces. Start anywhere — 7. works — and numbers are never rewritten. It must be the first thing on the line: 3.5 hours is just text.
=> ➔︎ Follow-up. A plain note or outcome leading from the line above.
=> @name or => (@name) ➔︎ @name Delegated. Owned by someone else — no action-state of its own. @name stays real, editable text (fix a typo directly); names may contain a hyphen, and (@a, @b, @c) delegates to several people, each badged.
=> # / v / > / x ➔︎ ☐︎ Consequence action. A follow-up that's itself an action, closed or reopened by clicking its glyph like any other. Mutually exclusive with delegating to a person.
# (topic) (topic) Topic tag. Groups actions by subject — recognised only right after a leading action symbol. The parentheses fade away until you edit the line.
! like this Emphasis. The rest of the line renders bold. Informational only, but searchable like any other line.
A line, then ==== underlined heading Section header. What Section History matches recurring sections by, and what a calendar sync matches meeting titles against.

Click a glyph to close an open action, or to reopen one that is done, deferred or won't-do. On a line with several actions (# call Sam => # book a room) a click changes exactly the glyph you clicked. From the keyboard the action nearest to the left of the caret is the one that changes (or, with none on the left, the nearest to its right), and on a line with none Ctrl+1–4 turn the whole line into that action.

Putting it together

How a week actually flows

1

Open today, type what's happening

ChronoNote always opens on today's note. Give a meeting a title and underline it to make a section, then log actions as they come up.

What you type
Daily Standup ============= - yesterday: fixed the sync retry bug # scope the Q3 roadmap doc
What you see
Daily Standup ============= yesterday: fixed the sync retry bug ☐︎ scope the Q3 roadmap doc
2

Resolve, delegate, or defer

Click a glyph to close an open action (or reopen a closed one), press Ctrl+Space to close the one at the caret, or Ctrl+1-4 to set any state directly. Hand something off with => @name — it stays a real, editable name, not a locked-in reference.

What you type
v scope the Q3 roadmap doc => @Priya loop in design on the sync UI
What you see
☑︎ scope the Q3 roadmap doc ➔︎ @Priya loop in design on the sync UI
3

Let recurring meetings remember themselves

Reuse the same section title tomorrow — "Daily Standup" again, or "1:1 — Priya" next week — and Ctrl+Shift+H pulls up every prior occurrence, aggregated, right from wherever your cursor sits in the current one.

4

Let your calendar fill itself in

Turn on "Sync calendar for this day" in Settings (off by default), and Ctrl+Shift+C reads a .agenda.json file kept up to date by whatever already syncs your real calendar, matches meeting titles against today's sections, and shows a review step before writing anything — new meetings are a checklist, and any section no longer on the agenda gets flagged for you to leave, discard, or move rather than silently dropped. A meeting the calendar marks as cancelled, declined or forwarded (a title starting with Canceled:, Declined:, Followed: and so on) counts as removed, named by the rest of its title, and Placeholder: or Confirmed - in front of a title is dropped. The review opens with focus on Sync; ↑ and ↓ move between the checkboxes.

5

Find anything, across every day

Ctrl+Shift+F searches every open tab or the whole notes folder at once. Results render the same glyph-aware way the editor does, so a search hit reads exactly like the real line, with the line before and after for context. Narrow it with is:open, is:done, tag:topic, has:@name, since:2026-09-01 or before:2026-09-20; each shows up as a chip you can click away.

6

Jot something that isn't dated yet

Ctrl+N opens a scratchpad — kept in memory only, never written to disk until you promote it into a real dated note. Closing one with real content unsaved asks first; nothing disappears by accident.

7

Keep lists as lists

Start a line with - for a bullet or 1. (or 2) , or 1.1. for a sub-item) for a numbered list, and Enter carries it on. An empty item ends the list; Tab nests it. Numbers are plain text and never rewritten, so nothing shifts under you when you rearrange a list.

8

Write without distraction, your way

Shift+F11 hides the bars for Zen mode (Esc brings them back). In Settings, set the editor's font size and line spacing, and switch on pure black in dark mode for OLED screens. The calendar dots in Ctrl+O show at a glance which days are all done (green), have open actions (amber) or are only notes.

Bringing notes in

Migrating from somewhere else

Already keeping notes in another tool? Settings → Data → "Import notes from a file…" brings them in, once they're converted to ChronoNote's plain-text format — one .txt file per day, tokens like # and => turned into actions and follow-ups.

The migration guide on GitHub spells out the exact file format a conversion script (or an AI agent) needs to produce, down to the token syntax and how recurring section titles get matched — precise enough to hand off directly rather than reverse-engineer from this page.

In the web app you can also drag a backup file or a day's .txt note onto the window. A note whose date already exists is never overwritten: if the text differs, you are shown both versions and choose to keep yours, use the dropped file, or keep both. If the web app's OneDrive sign-in expires (it can only be renewed for about a day), it says so and offers Sign in again — nothing is signed out and your unsynced edits are kept.

On your phone

ChronoNote on Android

The Android app is the same editor with the same tokens and the same plain .txt files, with a touch accessory bar above the keyboard for the symbols and actions. It syncs with a OneDrive folder, so your phone and your PC share the same notes.

Installing it

There's no store listing yet, so you install the APK yourself. It needs a 64-bit ARM phone (nearly every current phone) running Android 7 or newer.

  1. On the phone, open the latest release and download the file ending in _arm64.apk.
  2. Open the downloaded file. Android will ask you to allow installing apps from your browser or file manager — allow it, then install.
  3. To update later, download the newer APK and install it over the old one. Your notes and your OneDrive connection stay.

Connecting OneDrive

  1. Open Settings, go to the Notes & Sync tab, and under OneDrive Cloud Sync tap Connect Microsoft Account. Sign in in the browser that opens; you're returned to ChronoNote.
  2. Pick the OneDrive folder that holds your notes — the one your PC's OneDrive client syncs. The folder picker opens by itself once you're connected; Choose folder… reopens it.
  3. That's it. A spinner in the status bar shows a sync running, and Sync now in Settings starts one whenever you like.

ChronoNote syncs when you open the app, when you come back to it, and after you save a note. Notes are plain text on your phone and in OneDrive; the sign-in stays on the phone and is kept out of Android's cloud backup.

When the same note changed in two places

If you edited a note on the phone and it also changed in OneDrive, the two versions are merged line by line — additions from both sides are kept. Only when both sides changed the same lines does ChronoNote hold the note back instead of guessing. The status bar then shows ⚠ 1 sync conflict; tap it to see both versions and choose Keep this device's, Use OneDrive's or Keep both (the OneDrive text goes under a marker line at the end so you can tidy it). Nothing is overwritten until you choose, and no conflict copies are left lying around in your folder.

Deleting notes

Like on the desktop, closing a daily note you've emptied deletes it — and on Android that deletion syncs, so the empty file doesn't linger in OneDrive. A note deleted in OneDrive (for instance from your PC) disappears from the phone on the next sync.
