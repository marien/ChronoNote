Start here

Three things get you going. Everything else on this page is there when you want it.

  1. Give a meeting or topic a title and underline it with ===. That makes a section.
  2. Start a line with a mark: # for a to-do, => @name to hand something off, o for a topic to discuss.
  3. Press Ctrl+K for every other command. The palette shows each command's shortcut, so you learn them as you go.

It is all text. Type over a mark to change it, select and delete, paste from an email, undo. The glyphs follow. Open any note in Notepad and it reads exactly as you typed it: nothing is stored anywhere else.

The marks

Each mark is one to three characters, typed at the start of a line. On screen it becomes a glyph of exactly the same width, so nothing shifts while you type; in the file it stays what you typed.

Actions

# 
# call the printer
Open action. Something still to do. Closing a note dated today or earlier asks first while one of these is open; a note dated in the future closes without asking.
v 
v call the printer
Done.
> 
> call the printer
Deferred. Resolved for today, pushed forward to a later note. Drawn dimmed, like done and won't-do, so open work stands out.
x 
x call the printer
Won't do. Resolved, but not done: this one just isn't happening.

Follow-ups and people

=> 
=> they agreed on May
Follow-up. A note or outcome leading from the line above.
=> @name
=> @Joan finds referees
Handed off. Someone else owns it; no action state of its own. @name stays real text you can edit, names may contain a hyphen, and any @name anywhere on a line gets the same badge (an email address is left alone).
(@a, @b)
o coffee rota (@Ruth, @Ben)
Several people. Each name gets its own badge, on any line, agenda topics included.
=> # 
=> # send the minutes
Consequence action. A follow-up that is itself an action, closed by clicking its glyph like any other. Also => v, => > and => x.

Organising

# (topic)
# (market) stall rota
Topic. Groups actions in the action drawer and in search (tag:market). Recognised right after an action mark; the brackets show only while the caret is next to them.
by:fri
# order the kit by:{today+2}
Due date. Type by: and a day (fri, tomorrow, +3, wk44, 10-30) at the end of an action; when you leave the line it becomes the full date. Amber on the day, red once it has passed.
! 
! The pitch is ours until 16:00
Emphasis. The line is drawn bold. Informational, but searchable like any other line.

Meeting topics

o 
o spring tournament
To discuss. An agenda item, not a task: never counted as an action, never in the open-action jump.
. 
. spring tournament
Discussed. Dimmed like a closed action.
, 
, spring tournament
Not discussed. Skipped this time, carried to the next meeting. Forwarding a note turns an open o topic into this.

Structure

Title =====
Committee
=========
Section. A line underlined with =. Section history matches recurring sections by this title, and calendar sync matches meetings against it.
- * 
- bring the keys
Bullet. Purely structure, not an action. Indent two spaces to nest.
1. 2) 1.1. 
1. welcome and agenda
Numbered item. Plain text. Enter continues the numbering, an empty item ends the list, Tab nests it. It must be the first thing on the line: 3.5 hours is just text.
(>date#Section)
> call Sam (>{today+7}#Committee)
Link. Added when you forward an action; Ctrl+click to follow it. Any date in brackets works, so you can type one yourself.

Click a glyph to close an open action, or to reopen one that is done, deferred or won't-do. On a line with several actions (# call Sam => # book a room) a click changes exactly the glyph you clicked. From the keyboard, the action nearest to the left of the caret changes; on a line without one, Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+4 turn the whole line into that action.

Workflows

How the marks add up, roughly in the order you meet them in a week.

Write it down

ChronoNote opens on today's note the first time you start it each day. (Settings → Notes & Sync → Startup can bring back the tabs you had open instead; on the first start of a day today's note still opens when it has notes or meetings.) Give a meeting a title, underline it, and log what happens.

Nothing to fill in: a mark at the start of a line is all it takes, and you change it later by typing over it.

# (launch) check the analytics
=> @Priya owns the release notes

Close, defer or hand off

Click a glyph to close an open action, or to reopen a closed one. Ctrl+Space closes the one at the caret, and Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+4 set open, done, deferred or won't-do directly. Hand something off with => @name: it stays a name you can edit, not a locked-in reference.

The action drawer (Ctrl+Shift+A) lists the open actions of the open tabs or of every note, by date, topic, person or due date. On a wide window it opens beside the note so you keep typing; drag its edge to resize it.

v reply to the backlog
# check the analytics
=> @Joan finds referees
Open actions · 4
launch# check the analytics tournament# order the new kit market# print the posters

Let recurring meetings remember themselves

Reuse a section title (the committee again next month, "1:1 Priya" next week) and Ctrl+Shift+H shows every earlier occurrence, wherever your caret is in the current one. On a wide window it opens beside the note and follows you from section to section.

Select lines in an occurrence (↑/↓, Shift to extend, Ctrl+A for all) and choose Add to today or Add to next occurrence: the old lines are marked as forwarded, and whatever was deferred comes back open. As agenda reuses a whole past agenda with every topic open again.

Follow an action from day to day

Copy an open action and paste it into a later note, and the original is marked as forwarded. Both lines get a link: the old one to where it went, the new one to where it came from. Forward it again and the chain carries on.

A link shows as a quiet tag with an arrow; put the caret in it to see the text as typed. Ctrl+click opens that day at that section, and Ctrl+Alt+← takes you back. Ctrl+G jumps to any section of the open note.

The earlier note
> book the hall (>{today}#Committee)
Today's note
# book the hall (<{today-28}#Committee)

Give an action a date

End an action with by: and a day: by:fri, by:tomorrow, by:+3, by:wk44. When you leave the line it becomes the full date, so the file stays unambiguous. Click the date to change it in a small calendar, or press Ctrl+Shift+D.

The status bar counts late actions in all your notes, and the action drawer's By due date view lists them soonest first.

While you type
# order the new kit by:fri
After you leave the line
# order the new kit by:{friday}

Find anything, across every day

Ctrl+Shift+F searches the open tabs or the whole notes folder. Results look like the lines in your notes, with the line before and after for context. Narrow it with is:open, is:done, tag:tournament, has:@Joan, since:2026-09-01 or before:2026-09-20; each becomes a chip you can click away.

The action drawer's filter understands the same words, so tag:market since:2026-10-01 lists this month's open market actions.

has:@Joanis:open
{today-3}
=> # (tournament) ask @Joan for referees

Look back at the week

Ctrl+Shift+K shows one week, per day and per meeting: the actions still open, and how many are late. On a Monday it opens on last week, otherwise on this one; the arrow keys go a week back or forward. Turn on Show done to see what was finished as well.

Press Enter on a line to go there, or close or forward it right from the list. Open actions from earlier weeks are one click away at the top.

Lists and sub-actions

Start a line with - for a bullet or 1. (or 2) , or 1.1. for a sub-item) for a numbered list; Enter carries it on, an empty item ends it, Tab nests it. Numbers are plain text and only change when you press Enter in the middle of a list: the items after it shift down by one, up to the first blank line.

A list copied from Teams, Outlook, Word or a web page keeps its bullets, numbers and indentation when you paste it, and a list copied from ChronoNote arrives in those apps as a real list.

Indent actions under an action with Tab to make sub-actions: a thin line joins them, and the action drawer lists them under their action with a count such as 1/3. Steps in order can share one line: # onboard Lee => # laptop => # accounts. Closing an action never closes its sub-actions, or the other way round.

# spring tournament
  v book the pitches
  # (tournament) match schedule
  # ask @Joan for referees

Jot something that isn't dated yet

Ctrl+N opens a scratchpad. It is kept in memory only, never written to disk until you promote it into a dated note, and closing one with text in it asks first: nothing disappears by accident.

Write your way

Shift+F11 hides the bars for Zen mode (Esc brings them back). In Settings, set the editor's font size (or Ctrl+scroll in the note) and line spacing, choose colour or quiet grey glyphs, and switch on pure black in dark mode for OLED screens.

The date picker (Ctrl+O) shows at a glance which days have a note (bold), which have open actions (an amber ring) and, with calendar sync on, which only have meetings so far (a dashed ring), with ISO week numbers down the side; type wk 42 to jump to that week.

Spell check is off until you switch it on (Settings → Appearance). The desktop app uses the Windows spell checker: pick its languages, and right-click a marked word for suggestions, Add to dictionary or Ignore. In the web app your browser does the checking (Shift+right-click for its suggestions).

Let your calendar fill in the day

Turn on Sync calendar for this day in Settings (off by default), and Ctrl+Shift+C reads a .agenda.json file kept up to date by whatever already syncs your calendar. It matches meeting titles against the day's sections and shows a review before writing anything: new meetings are a checklist, and a section no longer on the agenda is flagged for you to leave, discard or move, never dropped silently.

A meeting the calendar marks as cancelled, declined or forwarded (a title starting with Canceled:, Declined:, Followed: and so on) counts as removed; Placeholder: or Confirmed - in front of a title is dropped. A small dot on the sync button shows when the open note differs from the calendar, and an empty note for today or later fills in its meetings straight away. A section starting with ', like 'Quick call with Dave, is never matched or flagged.

Something has to write that file: the .agenda.json reference describes it.

Take notes during a call

Ctrl+Shift+P turns ChronoNote into Peek: a small, see-through, always-on-top window that shows only the section your caret was in, so you keep looking at the people you are talking to. It is the same editor and stays inside its section; press the shortcut again (or Esc) to get the full window back as it was.

Ctrl+Alt+J works from any app, even while a call window has the focus, and opens Peek on the meeting that is on now according to your .agenda.json, or on a new 'Call 14:05 section placed in time order. Inside Peek, Alt+← / Alt+→ step through earlier and later occurrences of the section.

Peek is more solid while you type or point at it and fades a few seconds later; the opacities, fade time, header strip and shortcuts are in Settings. The dock button moves Peek into a solid strip along the bottom of the screen it is on; maximised windows, such as a Teams call, use the space above it. Drag its top edge to change the height.

Getting text back

ChronoNote keeps earlier versions of each note: one every few minutes while you edit today, then one per day, for 30 days. Right-click a tab and choose Earlier versions… to compare any of them side by side with the current text and restore it. The current text is kept as a version first.

A note you delete with text in it goes to Recently deleted (in Settings) for 30 days, where you can restore it. In the desktop app both are hidden folders inside your notes folder, so they travel with it; the web app keeps them in the browser's storage. Where everything is kept.

Export and print

Export a note or a date range as Markdown or standalone HTML from the More menu, the tab's menu or the command palette. Actions become - [ ] checkboxes, section titles become ## headings, and deferred or won't-do tasks keep their marks. Copy a selection as Markdown from the editor's context menu, or print the note (Ctrl+P) with the glyphs styled for paper.

Bringing notes in

Settings → Notes & Sync → Data → Import notes from a file… brings notes in once they are in ChronoNote's format: one .txt file per day, with marks like # and =>. The migration guide on GitHub describes the format precisely enough to hand to a conversion script or an AI assistant.

In the web app you can also drag a backup or a day's .txt file onto the window. A note for a date that already exists is never overwritten: when the text differs, you see both versions and choose to keep yours, use the dropped file, or keep both.

On your phone

The web app is the same editor with the same plain-text notes. A bar above the keyboard holds the marks and actions; the bar at the top shows the day and its open actions, with arrows to the previous and next note; the bar at the bottom takes you to the note, Actions, History and Search. Long-press a glyph for that line's menu. On the phone the week review opens from the date picker's week numbers.

Installing it

Open app.chrononote.mariendegelder.nl in your phone's browser. In Chrome on Android: menu (⋮) → Add to Home screen. In Safari on iOS: the Share button → Add to Home Screen. From then on the icon opens it full screen, and updates arrive the next time you open it.

Connecting OneDrive

  1. Open Settings → Notes & Sync, and under OneDrive tap Connect Microsoft Account. Sign in on the page that opens; you come back to ChronoNote.
  2. Pick the OneDrive folder that holds your notes, the one your PC's OneDrive syncs. The folder picker opens by itself; Choose folder… reopens it.
  3. That's it. A spinner in the status bar shows a sync running, and Sync now in Settings starts one.

ChronoNote syncs when you open the app, when you come back to it, and after you save a note. If the OneDrive sign-in expires, it says so and offers Sign in again; your unsynced edits are kept.

When a note changed in two places

Changes from both sides are merged line by line. Only when both sides changed the same lines does ChronoNote hold the note back, and the status bar shows ⚠ 1 sync conflict. Tap it to see both versions and choose Keep this device's, Use OneDrive's or Keep both (the OneDrive text goes under a marker line at the end). Nothing is overwritten until you choose.

Deleting notes

As on the desktop, closing a daily note you have emptied deletes it, and that deletion syncs. A note deleted in OneDrive disappears from your phone on the next sync.
