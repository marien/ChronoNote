## 404. Docked Peek with a docking station: screen changes, half-docked states, logging

**Status: released in v0.32.5 (beta 0.32.5-3 first).**

- **Marien (work laptop with a docking station, on 0.32.5-x):** docked Peek did not always switch to floating when the docking station was connected or disconnected; once the main window disappeared (quit from the taskbar); once the full window kept the docked strip's size after leaving Peek; entering Peek happens in visible steps and seemed able to stop half docked. No logging existed to see what happened.
- **Causes found by reading the code (not reproduced on a docking station; this machine has one monitor):** (1) while docked, every move Windows made was rewritten back onto the strip of the monitor Peek docked on, including the move Windows makes to take the window off a monitor that went away: the window was forced onto coordinates that no longer existed (off screen). (2) The screen check compared with the setup saved at the last reservation, and a move during the change made Peek reserve again and save the NEW setup first, so the display-change message that followed saw no difference. Windows' own AppBar notification was ignored. (3) Docking was several steps; a failed step left the window registered and held while the page believed it floated, and errors were swallowed; leaving Peek then had the leftover hold squeeze the restored window back to strip size.
- **Native (`peek_dock.rs`):** one screen check (`check_screens` / `dock_lost_reason`) on every signal: a move (judged by where it is going, so Win+Shift+Arrow to another monitor counts), WM_DISPLAYCHANGE, WM_DPICHANGED (a scaling change always floats: the strip's pixel height no longer fits), resume from sleep (WM_POWERBROADCAST), the AppBar notification ABN_POSCHANGED (otherwise the strip is taken again at the same height, the standard AppBar behaviour), end of a drag, restore from minimized. It compares the whole monitor set and the window's monitor; on a change it drops the hold at once and posts the undock (`peek-dock-lost` to the page). Docking is all or nothing (rollback on a failed reservation). The strip is also given back in WM_NCDESTROY, before an update install (the updater ends the process without the window's Destroyed event) and at process exit. New command `peek_dock_state` for the page.
- **Page (`peekWindow.ts`, `peek.ts`):** after every transition, when the dock is reported lost, and half a second after Windows moves or rescales the window while in Peek, `verify` compares the page's docked/floating with the native state and repairs a mismatch (releases a dock the page did not know about and re-places the window; floats when the page thought it was docked); it waits for an undock already on its way. A lost dock always goes through this check, so one arriving while Peek is still being entered is no longer dropped. Leaving Peek always releases a native dock, keeps the full window exactly where it was when its title bar is on a screen, pulls it in when not, and moves a window left off screen back. Errors in any step are logged instead of swallowed.
- **Logging:** app log lines carry milliseconds. Events (level PEEK: enter, dock, reserve, undock with its reason, float, leave, screen changes with monitor lists and work areas, repairs as WARN) go to `app.log` at once; details go to an in-memory flight recorder (`applog::detail`, last 400 lines; moves during the user's own drag are not recorded) written out (TRACE, in one write) when something goes wrong and when About -> Copy diagnostics is used, which now includes the last 300 log lines. Exit lines (`close requested`, `exit: window destroyed`, `exit: process ending`, `exit: installing an update`) and, at start, a warning when the previous run has no exit line (crash, killed, power loss). No note text is logged. Same in beta and stable builds.
- **Reviewed by Gemini 3.8 Flash** (cross-model review): nine findings, all valid, all fixed before merging (update install leaving the strip reserved, a lost dock dropped during entering, rectangles on leaving, a late repair after leaving, the move target, display scaling, sleep, restore order and destroy cleanup, the flight recorder flooded by drags).
- **Tests:** Rust `dock_lost_reason` (monitor set, order, moved/resized monitor, window on another monitor), log milliseconds, previous-run exit; Vitest: half-dock repairs in both directions, re-placing after a repair, leaving with an off-screen window or an unreachable title bar, failing steps logged; Playwright: a dock lost during entering is not dropped, the §398 test now goes through the check; native (`npm run test:native`, real app): a half dock made behind the page's back is found and released by the move check and leaving restores the full window, the log lines, exit lines and the killed-run warning. Not reproducible here: a real docking station, monitors with other scaling, sleep with an unplugged dock.

Confirmed by Marien (2026-10-26) on 0.34.0-4.

## 444. Outside changes to the open note keep the caret and the scroll position

## 457. Smarter numbered lists: nesting under the text, renumbering, sub-bullets

**Status: released in v0.37.2.**

Marien (2026-11-10): numbered lists should behave like a word processor's. Sub-items belong three spaces in (under the
text of `1. `), not two; indenting an item should start its numbering again; removing a number should renumber the
items after it; a numbered list can hold bullets. He approved a proposal of cases, built as proposed:

- **Tab** on a list line goes to the text column of the numbered item before it at the same indent (3 under `1. `,
  4 under `10. `, 5 under `1.2. `); with a bullet or nothing there it is the old +2. **Shift+Tab** goes back to the
  parent's indent. A selection of lines moves by the delta of its first list line.
- **Indenting** makes the item part of the group under its new parent: alone or first there it becomes `1`, otherwise
  it takes its place in their sequence and style (`2.1.` stays dotted). **Outdenting** continues the outer list; the
  items that followed it become its children, numbered from 1.
- **Backspace** right after the marker removes it and lines the text up under the previous item's text (the line
  continues that item); a second one takes it back to that item's indent.
- **Renumbering** keeps each group consecutive from the number its first item had before the edit (a list may start
  at 4), rewrites dotted prefixes to the parent's numbers and keeps the rest of the line byte for byte. Besides the

  number; with a bullet parent or none the line is cleared as before.
- **Clipboard (§439):** pasted lists nest under the parent's text (numbered) or 2 further (bullet); copying nests
  `<ol>`/`<ul>` by structure, so old 2-space and new 3-space lists both work. Nothing rewrites existing notes: every
  rule compares indents (smaller/larger), never exact widths.

## 464. Numbered lists: fixes from a cross-model review

**Status: released in v0.38.0 (beta 0.38.0-1 first).**

Marien (2026-11-13) asked Gemini 4 Hydrogen to review the numbered lists (§457, §462) for correctness, test
coverage and completeness. Its read-only review reported 10 correctness findings, the Tab-on-the-first-item edge
case Marien had seen (§457, details never given), §462 cases and test gaps; every correctness claim was reproduced
here before anything was built. Marien decided three behaviour questions (2026-11-14): Tab on the first item of a
list moves the whole list (as in Word), Backspace on an empty sub-item moves it out like Enter does, and a marker that
changes width (`9.` to `10.`) takes its sub-items and continuation lines along. The fixes, as a behaviour file of 15
rules with exact examples (`agent-tools/briefs/nl-fix-behaviour.md`, outside the repo):

1. Tab on the first item of a group moves the group with its subtrees (+2, or to the text column of a bullet right
   above); Shift+Tab on the first item of a parentless indented group mirrors it. This was the edge case: Tab added two
   spaces to one line and renumbered nothing.
2. Tab no longer looks past the item's parent (it could jump under a cousin's sub-item).
3. Enter on an item with sub-items renumbers the siblings after them (`renumberAfterInsert` stopped at the first
   deeper item); the sub-items follow the new item.
4. A split list: the first part keeps its start, later parts start at 1 (`matchGroupStarts`); pasting a blank line,
   moving a line into a list (Alt+Up/Down), typing over a multi-line selection and Enter on an empty middle item now
   renumber.
5. Typing a digit never renumbers, also on a blank line between two lists.
6. The second Backspace works past sub-items and bullets, and renumbers.
7. Backspace on an empty sub-item with a numbered parent moves it out (Marien's choice).
8. Enter on an empty sub-item: the sub-items after it become its children, from 1.
9. Shift+Tab on a selection restarts left-behind sub-items at 1.
10. `9.` to `10.` (and back) shifts the item's subtree (Marien's choice).
11. Tab-indented lists are compared by width (tab = 2) everywhere; the first Backspace keeps the tab.
12. Pasting onto an empty numbered item replaces the marker, as for `- ` and `# `.
13. Copying a list with continuation lines keeps one `<ol>` (continuations after a `<br>` in the `<li>`).
14. §462's `1.1.` rule counts only dotted items with a numbered parent and the same delimiter, and Tab in front of an
    existing dotted sub-group keeps it dotted.
15. Speed: groups in one pass with a parent stack (Tab in a 3,000-item list took 12 s, now well under 100 ms); the
    filter and commands read only the lines around the edit, not the whole document.
Also: spec.md no longer says Ctrl/Cmd+1-4 leave numbered items alone (they do not, §367).

Built by Gemini 4 Hydrogen (its first runs here) from a brief in two rounds: the first (24 min) passed Vitest but
could not run Playwright, and 22 of 155 list browser tests failed; the second (12 min, allowed to run the list specs)

## 465. Editing review, batch 1: no lost text

**Status: released in v0.38.0 (beta 0.38.0-1 first).**

Marien (2026-11-14) asked for a full review of the editor, the tokenizer and their surroundings, design and tests
included, because the numbered-list bugs (§464) and §463 shook his confidence in editing. Four Gemini 4 Hydrogen
reviewers each took one area (tokens and glyphs, editing keys, text in and out, the test suite); all 42 bugs they
reported were reproduced here or confirmed in the code. The report, with the bugs in four fix batches, twelve design
decisions (all approved by Marien the same day) and a test plan, is `docs/design/editor-review.html`. This first batch
fixes the five that could lose text or break the editor:

1. **Ctrl+Z never undoes an outside change** (decision D1). A disk reload (also a OneDrive sync), the phone companion
   and resolving an import conflict replaced the open note as an ordinary undo step: Ctrl+Z took the other version
   back and autosave made that permanent. `EditorApi.setContent(text, { outside: true })` now adds
   `Transaction.addToHistory.of(false)`; CodeMirror maps the earlier undo steps through it, so undoing one's own typing
   keeps the outside change. The app's own commands (`writeTabContent`, version restore, promote) stay undoable.
2. **Enter right after a leading symbol is a plain line break.** With the caret between a symbol and its space (the
   §463 case, `x|  `), Enter removed the "empty" symbol and the spaces with it; on `#| hello` it continued with `# `.
   `isCaretInLeadingSymbol` (tokens.ts, from the tokenizer) guards `actionLineEnter`, `topicLineEnter` and
   `bulletContinuation`.
3. **Closing a tab never overwrites a newer file.** `closeTab` started the save without waiting and cleared the tab's
   baseline hash first, so the save ran without its on-disk check. The close-time save is now `persistClosingTab`
   with a `closing` write guard that carries the baseline; a save of the same file still running at close counts as
   the tab's own (the chain of writes to one file remembers the hash of its last successful write), so it is not
   mistaken for someone else's change. A `tabBaseline` write whose tab closed while it waited in the chain falls back
   to that hash or the baseline it saw when queued, instead of writing without a check; a closed tab's save does not
   re-baseline a tab reopened on the file meanwhile; a pending autosave of a closed tab leaves the save to the close. A refused
   save keeps the text as a conflict copy, as for a background tab. Registered as in flight at once, so quitting right
   after a close waits for it.
4. **A failed phone-companion write keeps what was typed meanwhile.** `writeTabContentAndWait` reset the tab to the
   text before the phone's change; now only the phone's change is inverted and mapped through the typing
   (`ChangeSet.invert` / `map`).
5. **Spell check never draws results for text that changed** while the check ran (wrong underlines, or a RangeError
   that stopped the editor redrawing).

Built by Gemini 4 Hydrogen from a behaviour file (`agent-tools/briefs/b1-behaviour.md`, outside the repo), independent
browser tests written from the same file by a second Hydrogen run (`tests/e2e/editor-batch1.independent.spec.ts`, 55
tests; one `fixme`: the test backend cannot hold a save and then fail it, covered by the unit test). Reviewed here.

the close-time save fail and the last typing went to a conflict copy; a Hydrogen review of my version found four
more orderings of overlapping saves that went wrong, now each a unit test in `tabs.test.ts` that fails without the fix), and seven of the independent
tests that read the behaviour file too literally (the toast says "Saved your version as", the app's own last write

## 471. Editing review, decision D4: list keys behind a state symbol

**Status: released in v0.38.0 (beta 0.38.0-1 first).**

Decision D4 of the editor review (`docs/design/editor-review.html`), designed with Marien on 2026-11-14: the
numbered-list and bullet rules see through an action or topic symbol in front of the marker (`# 1. task`,
`v 2) done`, `o - point`), which until now made Tab a plain indent and left the numbers alone.

1. **One list:** `1. a`, `# 2. b`, `v 3. c` share one numbering. Setting or removing a state (Ctrl+1..7, Ctrl+Shift+O,
   the line menu) keeps the item in its list and changes no numbers; this reverses §467, where `1. task` to
   `# 1. task` left the list and the rest restarted.
2. **Tab / Shift+Tab:** `# 2. x` under `# 1. y` moves under the parent's text, `     # 1. x`, numbered from 1; all
   the existing Tab rules (continuing a group, dotted sub-items, whole groups) apply. A symbol never moves: an indent
   goes before it. Bullets with a symbol likewise (`o - detail` under `o - point` goes 4 spaces in).
3. **Enter:** on `v 2. x` gives `# 3. ` (a done, deferred or won't-do item continues as open, a topic as `o `); an
   empty item with a symbol ends the list or, as a sub-item, moves out as the parent's next number.
4. **Backspace** at the text start of `# 2. x` removes the number and keeps the symbol: `#    x`, its text under the
   item above's text, continuing that item, and the rest renumbers. A second Backspace gives `# x` at the list's
   indent, which splits the list (the rest restarts at 1), as for a plain continuation line.

Which lines split a list: a line with a symbol, no list marker and extra spaces after the symbol (`#    x`) continues
the list; `# call Sam` or `o Hiring` at the list's indent splits it, as any line there does. A first version had every
symbol line continue the list, which made Tab in one list renumber the next list under another topic heading; found
in review and fixed before merging.

Built by Gemini 4 Hydrogen from a behaviour file (`agent-tools/briefs/d4-behaviour.md`, outside the repo); independent
tests from the same file by a second Hydrogen run (`src/lib/editor/listLeads.d4.independent.test.ts`, 45 tests;
`tests/e2e/list-leads.d4.independent.spec.ts`, 5): all passed against the change except a selection detail (a bullet
with a symbol keeps the selection as a plain bullet does) and a browser helper that counted a glyph as two arrow steps.
Reviewed here.

## 475. Flat numbered outlines: `1.`, `1.1.`, `2.` at the start of the line again

**Status: released in v0.38.0 (beta 0.38.0-2 first).**

Marien (2026-11-15, on the 0.38.0-1 beta): an outline written by hand at the start of each line (`1. a`, `1.1. b`,
`2. c`) no longer worked; `1.1. b` became `2. b` on the next Enter. Found here: that changed in v0.37.2 (§457), not in
the 0.38 work. Before it (§201, v0.12.3) the app never renumbered, so a flat outline stayed as typed; §457's
renumbering read a list's levels from indentation only, so two items at the same indent were always siblings. Marien
chose to bring the flat style back, with rules approved the same day (`agent-tools/briefs/flat-behaviour.md`, outside
the repo):

- At the same indent and delimiter, an item with more number parts than the nearest numbered item above it is a flat
  sub-item of that item. Groups, renumbering (the prefix follows the parent), Enter (`1.1.` gives `1.2.`; an empty
  flat sub-item moves out as the parent list's next number), Backspace on an empty sub-item and the marker width rule
  (`9.` to `10.` never shifts a flat sub-item) all use that (`getGroupsInBlock`, `findParentInBlock` and
  `findParentInRun` in `src/lib/numberedLists.ts`).
- In a list with a flat sub-item, Tab and Shift+Tab change the level by number at the same indent (`1.2.` to `1.1.1.`,
  `1.2.` to `2.`), with the item's own sub-items; in other lists they indent as before (`numberedListKeys.ts`). The
  phone toolbar's Indent follows. A selection does this only when all its lines are numbered items of one flat list.
- Copying a flat outline as HTML nests the lists by level (`richClipboard.ts`).

Indented lists behave as before. Built by Gemini 4 Hydrogen; independent tests written from the behaviour file alone
by a second Hydrogen run (`src/lib/editor/flatLists.independent.test.ts`, 57 tests, and
`tests/e2e/flat-lists.independent.spec.ts`); 56 passed on the coder's branch at once, the 57th read rule 12 against
its words (the text lines up under the item right above, `1.1. b`) and was corrected. Reviewed here. Marien
confirmed the lists on the 0.38.0-2 beta (2026-11-15).

## 477. An outside change keeps the caret on its line when blank lines move

**Status: released in v0.38.0.**

The property test of outside changes (§470) failed on the 0.38.0-2 release commit with a random case: two blank lines
at the top of a note moved to the bottom (as a sync from another device can do) while the lines around the caret
stayed. `editorChanges` (`src/lib/editor/editorChanges.ts`), which turns an outside change into the smallest edit,
matched lines by longest common subsequence, and the two blank lines tied with the two kept text lines around the
caret; the tie went to the blank lines, so the caret's line counted as replaced and the caret jumped to a blank line.
The text itself was always right. Now a kept text line counts twice as much as a blank line, so the text lines win.
Test: a case in `editorChanges.test.ts` that fails without the fix.
