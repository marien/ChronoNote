F7 · Search finds decisions and sections

Four new words for Search (Ctrl+Shift+F) and the Actions filter, next to today's is:open, has:@dana, tag:, before: and due:. They combine with each other and with plain words.

WordFindsExample line it finds
section:reviewlines inside a section whose title contains the word (quotes for spaces: section:"design review"); times and dates in the title are ignored# send the mock-ups under [10:00 - 10:30] Design review
is:decisionfollow-ups that record an outcome: => text. budget => we keep the Q1 numbers
is:topicagenda topics in any state: o . ,o coffee rota (@Ruth, @Ben)
is:emphasislines marked !! the hall is booked until 22:00

"What did we decide about the budget in the board meeting?" becomes section:board is:decision budget.

Saved searches. A star button in the search field saves the query under its own text. Saved searches appear as chips under the field and in the palette (group "Saved searches"); right-click a chip to remove it.

7.1How section: matches a title

"Contains" lets section:review find "Design review" and "Budget review". "Whole title" needs section:"design review" but never catches a neighbour.

7.2What is:decision includes

Plain follow-ups: => we keep the Q1 numbers. Follow-up actions (=> # call Sam) are actions and already found by is:open; delegations (=> @dana) by has:@dana.

7.3Where saved searches are kept

In the notes folder (a small hidden file, .chrononote-searches.json), they follow you to the other laptop and the web app through OneDrive. On this computer only, they stay out of the folder.

F3 · Suggestions while typing

A small list under the caret offers what your notes already use, most recently used first, at most eight rows. Nothing is inserted unless you take a row; typing on narrows the list, and a space, Esc or a click elsewhere closes it. The note text never moves: the list floats over it.

What a row shows

The suggestion, and when you last used it. Names keep the case you wrote them with (@Ruth). If two spellings exist (@Ruth, @ruth), the one used most is offered.

With F9, the people invited to the meeting the caret is in come first, even before you have ever written their name.

3.1Which key takes a suggestion

If Enter always took the top row, typing @dan and Enter for a new line would turn it into @daniel. My pick: Tab takes it; Enter takes it only once you moved through the list with ↑/↓, otherwise Enter is a normal Enter.

3.2When the list appears for names

After the first letter, a lone @ (in an address, a handle) shows nothing. Right after @, you see your recent people at once.

3.3Which section titles are offered

Recurring: used at least twice in the last 90 days, plus today's calendar meetings. That is what Section History can follow; one-off titles would fill the list with noise.

3.4The phone

On the phone the list would compete with the keyboard and the accessory bar. My pick: build it for desktop and the web app, then decide on the phone once you have used it.

F5 · Person view

Everything about one person in one place before you meet them, read-only. Ctrl+click on an @name, the line menu's "Show @Dana", or @dana in the palette (people listed below the dates) opens it. A row jumps to its line; Esc closes.

It finds the name however it was written (@dana, @Dana, (@Dana, @Ben)), but not a longer name: @dan is someone else.

5.1Where it opens

Beside the note on a wide window, like Section History, so you can keep typing while you look; a dialog on a narrow window and the phone. A dialog always is simpler, but covers the note.

5.2How far back delegations and mentions go

Delegations have no done state, so "everything" grows forever. Open actions are always all shown, however old.

5.3How to open it

Ctrl+click is quickest with the mouse. A plain click keeps placing the caret, as now.

F6 · Rename across all notes

Rename a section title, an @name or a (topic) in every note at once. Start from the line menu on the thing itself ("Rename everywhere…") or the palette. A dialog shows every line that will change before anything is written.

Rename the section "Coffee rota" to "Hospitality rota"
From  Coffee rota              To  Hospitality rota
14 lines in 14 notes

☑ 2026-11-13   [10:00 - 10:30] Coffee rotaHospitality rota
☑ 2026-11-06   [10:00 - 10:30] Coffee rotaHospitality rota
☑ 2026-10-30   Coffee rotaHospitality rota - 2026-10-30
…
                                     [ Cancel ]  [ Rename in 14 notes ]
  • A title matches as Section History matches it: a time in front or a date at the end are kept, only the title itself changes.
  • A name matches the whole name in any case (@dana, @Dana), and a topic only as a tag after an action. The new text is written as you typed it.
  • Before a note changes, it gets an earlier version (Earlier versions… in the tab menu has it). An open note changes in the editor, as one undo step. A note that changed on disk while the dialog was open is skipped and named afterwards.
6.1Untick single notes in the preview

Useful when one old "Coffee rota" really was a different meeting.

6.2Undo the whole rename at once

"Renamed in 14 notes · Undo" puts every note back from the version saved just before, unless you changed it since. Without it, you restore note by note from Earlier versions.

6.3Section titles in notes dated after today

Calendar sync owns those: it matches sections to meetings by title, so renaming them would make it flag the section as removed and add the meeting again. Names and topics change in every note either way.

F9 · A richer calendar file

Three optional fields in .agenda.json. A file without them works exactly as today. What is worth building depends on what your exporter can write, so the first question is yours to answer from that side.

{ "date": "2026-11-17", "start": "10:00", "end": "10:30", "title": "Design review",
  "joinUrl": "https://teams.microsoft.com/l/meetup-join/…",
  "attendees": ["Dana Smith", "Ben de Vries"],
  "seriesId": "AAMkAGI2…" }
FieldWhat ChronoNote does with it
joinUrlA Join button on Peek's "on now" chip and in its on-now list (Ctrl+G in Peek). It opens the link in your browser; ChronoNote watches nothing. Only https: links.
attendeesThe people of the meeting the caret is in come first in the @ suggestions (F3).
seriesIdSection History follows a series through a title change. Needs a small side file that remembers which title belonged to which series.
9.1What your exporter can supply

Answer from what the work-side export (Outlook via your Power App or script) can read. "None" parks F9.

9.2How an attendee becomes an @name

"Dana Smith" becomes @Dana; two Danas become @Dana and @Dana-S. Or the exporter writes exactly the name you use in notes, which only it can know.

9.3Series through a title change

F6 already joins a renamed series in one step, by hand, when it happens. The series id would do it by itself, at the cost of a side file to keep in step.
