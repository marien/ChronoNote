ChronoNote Feature Review

Three review rounds made ChronoNote solid: the code (v0.27, v0.28), the design (v0.30) and readiness for other people (v0.31). This one looks at what it does for you during a working day. It follows the day from start to finish: starting the day, meetings, actions and the people you hand them to, writing and finding, capturing from elsewhere, and closing the week. Each card says what happens now, what I propose, and what it would cost.

Status on 25 October 2026

  • Released in v0.32.0 (14 October): the whole D section. D1 Replace, D2 spell check, D3 Go to section and D4 date links, with Back and Forward and forward links on every way of forwarding (CHANGELOG §369-§378).
  • Released in v0.33.0 (25 October): B2 due dates (§419-§422).
  • Built alongside, not on this page: sub-actions and numbered steps, joined by thin lines in the note, nested and foldable in the Action Drawer, and drawn the same in Section History (§423-§427, v0.33.0); forward links drawn as a small tag with an arrow (§428-§429, v0.33.0); "Last note" keeping your tabs on every start (§430, v0.33.1).
  • Still open: A1, A2, A3, B1, B3, C1, C2, E1, E2. Their decision buttons below still work; the released cards show what shipped instead.
Plain text stays plain textAnything new is written as readable text in the note, or kept out of the note entirely. No hidden IDs, no database.
Keyboard firstEvery proposal works without the mouse. New shortcuts avoid function keys and the AltGr letters.
Nothing leaves the machineNo accounts, no telemetry, no AI services, no watching other apps.
Quiet while writingNothing pops up while you type. New things appear when you ask for them, or once at a natural moment such as the first note of the day.

Already there

For calibration: these cover a lot of the daily flow already, and the proposals build on them.

  • Actions with four states, topics with three, delegation with @name, topic tags.
  • Copy/paste forwarding, and Shift+Enter in Actions to forward one action to today.
  • Calendar sync from .agenda.json, with a review of removed meetings.
  • Section History with "As agenda", Copy to next occurrence, occurrence navigation.
  • Peek for calls, with the call shortcut that opens the meeting that is on now.
  • Search with is:, tag:, has:@, since: and before:; the command palette; Ctrl+J between open actions.
  • Export, print, Recently deleted, Earlier versions.
  • The web app on a phone, sharing notes through OneDrive.

The gaps found:

  • Starting the day is manual. Yesterday's open actions stay behind until you forward them one by one, and every new note starts empty.
  • Delegated work drops out of sight. => @name lines have no state, so they can't be marked done. Actions hides them unless you type @, and nothing lists what you're waiting on per person.
  • Nothing has a date it's due. An action written for Friday looks the same as one for next month.
  • Meetings are prepared and reported by hand. Postponed topics only reach the next occurrence if you copy them, and there's no clean summary to send afterwards.
  • Small editor basics are missing: replace, spell check, a jump list of the note's sections, and links from one note to another.
  • Capturing needs ChronoNote in front. A thought during other work means switching windows. On the phone, a link or text from another app has no way in.

A · Starting and closing the day

What happens before the first meeting and after the last.

A1

Bring open actions forward at the start of the day

Now

Open actions stay in the note where they were written. To carry them to today you copy and paste them (the source becomes > ), or forward them one at a time with Shift+Enter in Actions. After a few days the open work is spread over several notes, and Ctrl+J only walks through the current one.

Proposed

A command "Bring open actions forward" (palette, the More menu, a shortcut such as Ctrl+Shift+B) opens a review list of every open # from earlier notes, grouped by date, all ticked. Enter moves the ticked ones to the top of today's note under a "Carried over" heading and marks each source > , the same rule as forwarding now. Optional setting: show this once when today's note is first opened, and only if there is something to bring.

It's the daily chore the app already does for one action at a time. The review list keeps you in control: nothing moves until you confirm.

A2

A template for new daily notes

Now

A new day starts empty. Calendar sync fills in the meetings, but a fixed part of every day (a "Today" list, a standing "Inbox" section, a reminder line) is typed again each morning.

Proposed

An optional plain-text file in the notes folder, .chrononote-template.txt, is copied into every new dated note before calendar sync adds the meetings. Optional per-weekday files (.chrononote-template-mon.txt) for days that look different. Settings shows whether a template is in use and opens the file. No template file means today's behaviour.

The template syncs with the notes through OneDrive, so the web app uses the same one.

A3

Week review

Now

Seeing what happened this week means opening five notes, or searching with since:. A status update for a manager is put together by hand.

Proposed

A read-only "Week review" view (palette, date picker week number): per day, what was done, what was decided (=> lines), which topics were discussed, and what is still open or waiting on someone. Arrow keys move between weeks; Enter jumps to the line. "Copy as Markdown" puts a tidy summary on the clipboard. Nothing is written to any note.

B · Actions and people

What you owe, what others owe you, and by when.

B1

Waiting on: delegated work you can close

Now

=> @dana send the slides is a delegated action with no state of its own. Actions hides these lines unless the filter contains @. There is no way to mark one as received or done short of deleting the arrow, and no list of what each person still owes you.

Proposed

Delegated lines get the same states as your own, written in the existing consequence-action form: => # @dana send the slides is waiting, => v @dana … is received, => x @dana … is dropped. Plain => @dana keeps meaning "waiting" so old notes stay valid. Actions gets a "Waiting on" switch that groups open delegated lines by person, oldest first, and Ctrl+Space closes one in place. Delegated lines stay out of your own open count and don't block closing a tab.

This is the largest gap in the meeting workflow: an action leaves your list the moment it goes to someone else. Your call because it settles what a delegated line means.

B2

Due dates written in the line

Now

An action has no date other than the note it's in. "Before Friday" lives in the text, and nothing notices when Friday passes.

Proposed

An optional by: token at the end of an action, using the date words search already knows: # send the report by:2026-10-17, typed as by:fri and rewritten to the full date when you leave the line (so the file stays unambiguous). The editor shows it as a small date pill: muted, then amber on the day, red once it has passed. Actions sorts and filters on it (due:today, due:week, overdue); the status bar counts overdue items. No notifications (see the "left out" list).

Your call: it's new syntax in your notes. With A1 and B1 it covers most of what a separate task app would add.

What shipped · v0.33.0 · §419-§422

by: with a date or a word (by:fri, by:tomorrow, Dutch words too), rewritten to the full date when you leave the line, or Ctrl+Shift+D for a compact date picker; a click on the date opens it too. Instead of a pill, the date is drawn in place: by: becomes a small flag and one outline surrounds flag and date, muted, amber on the day, red once past, so the line keeps its width. The status bar counts overdue actions across all notes. Instead of due: filters, the Action Drawer has a "By due date" view: Overdue, Today, This week, Later. Delegated lines can carry a date but stay out of your own count.

B3

Search filters in Actions

Now

Cross-tab Search understands tag:, has:@, since: and before:, shown as removable chips. The Actions filter is plain text only, so "open actions about the budget from this month" isn't possible there.

Proposed

The Actions filter uses the same parser and chips as Search. tag:budget since:2026-10-01 works the same in both places, and typing @dana keeps today's meaning (show that person's delegated lines).

The parser and chips exist already. This is mostly wiring and tests.

C · Meetings

Before, during and after a recurring meeting.

C1

Carry unfinished topics into the next meeting

Now

When calendar sync creates a section for a recurring meeting, it starts empty. Topics that were still open (o ) or postponed (, ) last time, and open actions under that meeting, only arrive if you copy them over with Section History's "As agenda" or Copy to next occurrence.

Proposed

A setting under Calendar: "Fill new meeting sections from the last occurrence". When sync adds a section, it brings the open and postponed topics from the previous occurrence as o and marks them carried in the source, the same rule as "As agenda". Open actions are listed under a "From last time" line. The sync review shows what will be carried, so you can untick items.

Section History already does this by hand; this runs the same rule at the moment the meeting appears.

C2

Copy a meeting as a summary

Now

Export copies a whole note as Markdown. Sending a summary of one meeting means selecting the section and cleaning up the symbols by hand.

Proposed

"Copy section as summary" in the line menu, on the section title and in the palette. It puts a short plain-text summary on the clipboard, ready to paste into a mail or Teams: the title and date, then Decisions (=> lines), Actions with their owner (@name, or "me"), Discussed and Postponed topics. Loose notes are left out.

D · Writing and finding

Editor basics that a Notepad replacement is expected to have.

D1

Replace in the current note

Now

Ctrl+F finds, but there's no replace. Renaming a person or a project in a long note is done match by match.

Proposed

Ctrl+H (as in Notepad) opens the find bar with a Replace field: Replace and Replace all, match case and whole word. One undo step reverts a Replace all. Only the current note; renaming across notes stays out.

CodeMirror's search already supports replacing; the find bar needs the field and the keys.

What shipped · v0.32.0 · §369

As proposed: Ctrl+H opens the find bar with a Replace row (a chevron shows or hides it, as in Notepad), Replace and Replace all, match case and whole word, one undo step for Replace all.

D2

Spell check (off by default)

Now

No spell check. WebView2 has Windows' own spell checker built in, but the editor turns it off.

Proposed

A setting under Editor, off by default. When on, misspelled words are underlined using the Windows input languages (Dutch and English together for you). Suggestions appear at the top of the line menu on right-click. @names, topic tags, URLs and tokens are skipped.

Optional: in quick meeting notes the squiggles may bother you more than they help.

What shipped · v0.32.0 · §370, §375-§378

Off by default. Built on the Windows spell checker itself rather than WebView2's (which lost its marks in the editor), with its own underlines, suggestions plus Add to dictionary and Ignore in the line menu, and a language picker in Settings (default: your Windows languages plus English). The web app uses the browser's checker.

D3

Go to a section in the note

Now

A day with eight meetings is a long note. Reaching the fourth section means scrolling or searching for its title.

Proposed

Ctrl+G opens a short list of the note's sections, each with its open-action count. Type to filter, Enter to jump. The same list in the palette with a # prefix, so Ctrl+K then #stand lands on the standup.

What shipped · v0.32.0 · §369

As proposed: Ctrl+G opens the palette on # with the note's sections and their open-action counts; # typed in Ctrl+K does the same.

D4

Links to other days and sections

Now

"See the notes from 3 October" is plain text. URLs are clickable with Ctrl+Click, but dates are not.

Proposed

A full date in the text (2026-10-03) is a link: Ctrl+Click or middle-click opens that day's note, as with URLs. Written as 2026-10-03#Standup, it opens that day at that section. Nothing changes in the file; the date is the link. The calendar's "has a note" styling shows whether the target exists.

Optional: useful when you refer back often, otherwise invisible. No new syntax beyond the date you'd type anyway.

What shipped · v0.32.0 · §371-§374; pills §428-§429 (v0.33.0)

A full date is a link (Ctrl+Click, middle-click); a date with a section is a link in brackets: (2026-10-03#Standup). Added on your request: Back and Forward (Ctrl+Alt+Left/Right, the mouse buttons, a small toast), and forward links: forwarding an open action writes (>date#section) on the source and (<date#section) on the copy, on every way of forwarding. Since v0.33.0 these show as a small tag with an arrow (→ went to, ← came from).

E · Capture from anywhere

Getting a thought into today's note without ChronoNote in front.

E1

Quick add from anywhere

Now

Writing something down while in another app means switching to ChronoNote, finding today's tab, and finding the right place. The call shortcut (Ctrl+Alt+J) helps only during a meeting.

Proposed

A second system-wide shortcut, Ctrl+Alt+K (no AltGr letter on K; changeable), opens a one-line box on top of whatever you're doing. Type the line, press Enter: it's added to today's note as an open action, or under an "Inbox" section if your template (A2) has one. Shift+Enter adds it as a plain line, Escape cancels. The box goes away and focus returns to the app you were in.

It reuses the global-shortcut binder and the small-window code from Peek, and only listens to its own key; it doesn't watch other apps.

E2

Share to ChronoNote on the phone

Now

The installed web app can't receive anything from other apps. A link or a piece of text has to be copied, then pasted into today's note.

Proposed

Register the installed web app as a share target. ChronoNote then appears in the phone's Share sheet; shared text or a link is added to today's note as an open action and synced through OneDrive as usual. Android only: iPhones don't let web apps receive shares.

Considered and left out

  • Reminders and notifications for due dates: they need the app running and would interrupt; B2 shows overdue work where you already look.
  • Recurring actions: recurring meetings already come from the calendar, and A1 carries open work forward. A repeat engine would need hidden state.
  • Separate projects or task lists: topic tags and search already group work, and daily notes stay the single place to write.
  • Sharing notes with other people, accounts, collaboration.
  • AI summaries: they would send your notes to an outside service.
  • Images, attachments, rich text: they break the plain-text premise.
  • Detecting calls or watching other apps: abandoned on 5 October, and stays that way.
